# Pitch — for somebody who wants steady, not exceptional

The companion to `pitch-reaching-past-the-average.md`, and the rules in that file's notes apply
here unchanged. This one is for the person who does not want their life to be a campaign, and it
must never read as the consolation version. The arithmetic is the same arithmetic. What differs
is the rate somebody picks, and picking a small one is not picking a small outcome.

---

## Quora

Five dollars.

Not an hour. Not a month. Five dollars, once, from one person, across a year.

That is the smallest figure anybody puts into this, and it comes to a quarter of a million,
because one percent of five million people is fifty thousand of them.

I am writing this down because the case for all of this usually sounds like it is addressed to
people who intend to be extraordinary, and most people do not intend that. Most people want
enough, steadily, without every week being a push. That is a real goal and the arithmetic is
indifferent to which one you picked. It is the same arithmetic either way.

What the arithmetic is not indifferent to is whether anything sits underneath the figure. Who
pays you first. What is actually stopping that, concretely — the one thing, not the general
condition. What gets done about it this week, and whose name is against it.

There is a post on the blog from last May that works this through without a calculator in sight.
It takes three ordinary trades — somebody caring for a neighbor, somebody fixing cars, somebody
watching other people's children — puts a weekly number of hours against each, and follows what a
few hundred people trading with each other would come to over a year. Twenty-five hours a week.
Thirty. Fifteen. Nothing in it asks anybody to be remarkable. It is at
https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/From-62-to-384%3A-Building-a-Real-Economy-Where-We-Live%2C-Work%2C-and-Prevail
and it is worth the few minutes before you decide this is not for you.

Working that out is my trade. Thirty minutes, eight questions in a fixed order, and you finish
with one action and a name on it rather than a list. A voice assistant runs the call; I read what
comes out of it afterwards, and then there are two of us on it instead of one.

It does not end when the call does. It ends when you have a paying customer.

The economy that pushed you out has headhunters — people whose job is to pitch you, place you,
and take a percentage for it. This one has nobody standing in the middle of a trade, deliberately,
and that is the right way to build it. It does leave a gap: nobody is working on your side unless
somebody decides to. I decided to. There is no cut of what you make.

## Notes for whoever edits this next

- Never set this template against the other one, and never call this goal modest, realistic,
  humble or achievable. Each of those words tells somebody they aimed low.
- Five dollars is the calculator's own lowest row, not a recommendation and not a floor on what
  anybody should charge. Do not let a line drift into advising a rate.
- Everything in the other template's notes applies here: no ServiceCredits, no forecast framing,
  no May figures, no perp language, nothing about a person who has taken a call.
- The linked post states its yearly figures as dollar amounts in ServiceCredits, which the
  Dictionary no longer permits. It stands as record and is not edited for that. This template
  therefore links it and describes it — the trades, the hours, the shape of the case — and quotes
  no figure from it. Keep it that way. Naming one of those numbers here would carry the framing
  out of the record and into current copy.
- The link is to the blog, not to Quora. The same writing exists as a raw export awaiting a copy
  edit; that copy is not a second post and is not a link target.
- The blog link sits inside a sentence on purpose. Quora turns a bare URL alone on its own line
  into a preview card, and only one link here should get a card: the page. Do not tidy the link
  onto a line of its own.
