# Prompt for Replit — the screens that have no design yet

Copy everything below this line.

---

You designed the operator desk for One Percent on the `design/ops-command-center` branch.
That one is done and is being built. Do not redo it. This is about the screens you have not
drawn, which is most of the product.

## What One Percent is

One person sells one thing: a paid half-hour voice call about what somebody would charge one
person, once, and what it would take to get a first paying customer. It costs $7, real money.
After the call the operator reads it and writes that person a sheet — things worth doing, in
their own situation. That sheet is the product. Everybody who calls gets one, whichever way
the decision went.

Nobody needs an account to pay or to have the call. An account is only for afterwards, to come
back and read the sheet.

## Rules that are not up for redesign

- **Dark only.** One theme. No light mode, no switch.
- **One phone-width column at every screen size.** Centred on anything wider. There is no
  desktop layout and none is wanted.
- **Nobody has a name.** A person is a reference code, like `H8TF7-AFJYL`. There are no
  names, no photographs and no avatars with initials, because there is nothing to put in
  them. Do not design around a person's identity.
- **There is no chat anywhere.** No message box, no reply form, no contact address, on any
  screen. It existed, it was removed on purpose, and it is not coming back. Somebody who
  wants to speak again buys another call.
- **Real money is money.** $7, and a quote in dollars, are money and are written as money.
- **Copy carries no pleasantries.** No thanks, no apologies, no "we're excited", no first
  person feelings, no marketing language. Plain statements.
- Same visual language as the command center you already made: the same palette, the same
  serif-for-numbers-and-names against sans-for-labels, the same card radii and hairlines.

## Screens to design

Each one needs every state listed, not just the full one.

### 1. Pay for a session

The first screen somebody sees. It carries the price, two ways to pay — a Wise handle for
somebody with a bank account, and a gift card code for somebody without one — and gives back
a reference code plus a claim link the buyer keeps.

States: before choosing a route; the Wise route; the gift card route; the reference and claim
link handed over; the buyer has lost their link and is recovering it from the reference or the
card code.

### 2. Your session — the claim page

The page somebody keeps and comes back to. It is the only thing that links them to what they
paid for, and it works with no account.

States: reading the order; paid but not matched yet; matched, with a control to start the
call; the call running; the call ended; how many minutes are left of the thirty; a start that
failed and what to do; an offer to sign in and keep the session against an account.

This page is somebody about to be spoken to for half an hour. It should read like that.

### 3. Signed out, and signing in

Signing in grants nothing on its own. Somebody who signs in without a session sees a page
saying so. Design that, and the sign-in itself.

### 4. What somebody sees once they are signed in

Their own sessions, with a state on each: paid and not started, started and nothing came back,
called and waiting to be read, read. The sheet they were written, read in full. A form to paste
the claim link if the session is not linked yet. Any quotes that have been written for them,
with what each covers.

No way to write anything. The sheet is read, not replied to.

### 5. Payments — the operator confirming money arrived

A separate operator screen from the desk. What is waiting to be confirmed, with a control to
confirm or decline each one. A way to find a single reference. Everything confirmed so far.

### 6. The parts of the desk you skipped

The command center covers the queue, a person's header, the decision, the transcript, the path
and its milestones, and the case history. These are on the same screen and have no design:

- **What is owed on a person.** An ordered list of what still has to happen to them, at the
  top of their record: keep the call record, decide, write the sheet. It is a worklist, not a
  set of controls that happen to be on a screen.
- **The sheet.** Writing it, editing it later, and seeing when it was written. This is the
  deliverable and it is the thing the mockup has no design for.
- **Quotes.** The operator writes one for one person: an amount in dollars and what it covers.
  It has a state — written, agreed, declined.
- **Introductions.** Two reference codes put in touch, why, and what came of it. It appears on
  both people's records and reads the same from either end.
- **Where it ends.** The work ends when that person has somebody paying them. One recorded
  fact, reversible.
- **How far people get.** Five figures, each a strict subset of the one above it: calls that
  came back, worth going on with, on a path, something worked, first paying customer.
- **More than one call against one order.** A session that dropped and was restarted is two
  calls. Only the ones that came back are shown.
- **Exporting the call script** the voice agent runs.

### 7. The states nothing has been drawn for

Loading, empty, and failed, on every screen above. A failure says what failed and what to do
about it, not "something went wrong".

## What to do with anything new

If a screen seems to need a capability that is not described here, design it and mark it
clearly as a proposal. Do not fold it in as though it exists. The same goes for the controls
on the command center that were never in the product — the search box, the queue filters, the
notifications bell, desk preferences, the live daily counter, the assessment scorer, the
relationship graph and the drafted reply. They are proposals, not built.
